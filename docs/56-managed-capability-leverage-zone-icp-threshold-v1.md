# Managed Capability Leverage Zone / ICP Threshold v1

Status: BUSINESS HYPOTHESIS / ICP VALIDATION — NOT PRICE DECISION
Date: 2026-09-13

## 1. Purpose

Determine where FACTACT + Shared Capability can create an economic advantage over conventional internal staffing / onsite support / work-transfer BPO.

This document does NOT decide a final ICP, price, employee-count band, or guaranteed compression rate.

### External evidence
Public Japanese surveys show a strong relationship between company scale and IT investment behavior. A METI-related survey reported IT investment implementation rates of 37.3% for <=50 employees, 46.6% for 51–100, 58.6% for 101–300, 74.8% for 301–1,000, and 94.2% for >1,000. This supports using company scale as a meaningful commercial/IT-environment proxy, but it does not prove any atLIB price or ICP.

Recent ITR research also explicitly analyzes IT investment by employee bands (<300, 300–999, 1,000–4,999, 5,000+), supporting the proposition that company scale is a material dimension of IT economics.

## 3. Key correction

Do not ask:

> How many hours can atLIB sell for ¥498k / ¥600k / ¥800k?

Ask:

> At what customer scale and complexity does the economic value of shared IT capability grow faster than atLIB's incremental Human Work?

This is the Leverage Zone.

## 4. Leverage Zone model

Candidate model:

Customer IT Requirement
= Scale × Complexity × Workload × Responsibility × Service Requirement

atLIB Delivery Requirement
= Managed Scope × Human Work Structure × (1 - Valid FACTACT Leverage) × Role Cost + Non-Human Delivery Cost

Commercial Value
= Alternative IT Function TCO + Avoided Work + Risk/Continuity Value + IT Investment Decision Value + Improvement Value

A strong Leverage Zone exists where:

1. Customer Required IT Capability rises materially,
2. customer cannot economically maintain all specialist capabilities internally,
3. repeated/structured Work is large enough for FACTACT leverage,
4. the environment is not dominated by synchronous L1 human interaction or physical onsite work,
5. atLIB Human Work grows slower than customer Scale / Complexity / Value,
6. commercial value materially exceeds sustainable Delivery COGS.

## 5. Why company size matters but cannot be the only ICP variable

Employee count can correlate with:
- account/device population
- SaaS/system count
- department and approval complexity
- number of vendors
- security/governance requirements
- IT investment size
- business impact of incidents
- required specialist capability

But exceptions are expected.

A standardized 500-person cloud-native company may be easier to operate than a 150-person multi-site legacy environment.

Therefore:

> Company Size = useful proxy / qualification input.
> Actual Scale + Complexity + Work Structure = Assessment FACT.

## 6. Candidate company-scale zones — HYPOTHESIS

### Zone A: ~50 employees
Possible characteristics:
- relatively small IT estate
- limited specialist requirement
- owner/admin/generalist-led IT
- smaller IT investment base

Potential issue:
The absolute value pool may be too small for a broad Managed Capability unless the customer has unusually high complexity or regulatory needs.

Likely fit:
- Diagnosis / Assessment
- focused KAIZEN Project
- narrower Managed Scope

Not a rejection. Complexity can override size.

### Zone B: ~100 employees
Possible characteristics:
- IT ownership begins to become a persistent organizational issue
- SaaS/device/account operations become meaningful recurring Work
- one-person IT / concurrent-role bottlenecks can emerge
- specialist capability remains difficult to hold internally

Potential leverage:
FACTACT standardization + shared specialist capability may begin to become economically meaningful.

### Zone C: ~300 employees
Possible characteristics:
- substantially larger device/account/system landscape
- multiple departments and approval paths
- more vendor coordination
- stronger security/governance requirements
- meaningful IT investment and business dependency
- one or a few generalists cannot cover all required capability well

Hypothesis:
This may be a particularly strong Leverage Zone for Managed Capability because Required Capability is broad while a fully specialized internal IT organization can be expensive.

This is NOT yet a DECISION that 300 employees is the ICP.

### Zone D: ~500 employees
Possible characteristics:
- broader specialist and governance requirements
- higher business impact and responsibility
- potentially larger value pool

Counter-force:
- customer may already have a larger internal IT department
- enterprise procurement / SLA / compliance requirements may increase atLIB Delivery COGS
- more custom/legacy systems can reduce standardization leverage

Therefore larger does not automatically mean better ICP.

## 7. Candidate Leverage Curve

HYPOTHESIS:

Customer Scale / Complexity increases
→ Required IT Capability increases rapidly
→ Internal specialist staffing cost increases
→ Shared Capability advantage increases
→ BUT after a point enterprise-specific complexity / SLA / dedicated staffing may also increase rapidly.

Therefore the expected ICP is not necessarily the largest customer.

Conceptually:

Low Scale
  low value pool / limited need
        ↓
Emerging Leverage
  IT burden exceeds generalist capacity
        ↓
High Leverage Zone
  broad capability needed, but full internal specialist organization inefficient
        ↓
Enterprise Complexity Zone
  dedicated/custom requirements may reduce shared-model leverage

The exact boundaries are UNKNOWN.

## 8. Human Work Structure by scale

Do not model 100h as homogeneous.

Human Work should be decomposed by:
- Routine Execution
- User Interaction
- Engineering
- Specialist / Security
- Decision / Approval
- Vendor Coordination
- Management / Governance
- Evidence / Verification
- Search / Context Gathering
- Duplicate Administration / Reporting

and by:
- Skill Level
- Decision Level
- Risk Level
- Interaction intensity
- Exception rate

Hence:

> Human Work Hours ≠ Human Work Cost ≠ Human Work Complexity ≠ Customer Value.

## 9. FACTACT economic advantage

FACTACT leverage should be tested not only as Hours Compression but as Human Work Structure Transformation.

Desired direction:
- duplicate administration ↓
- context search ↓
- reporting-only work ↓
- repeated specialist investigation ↓ through Knowledge reuse
- routine execution → standardization / automation
- Human attention shifts toward decision, exception handling, improvement, and high-value interaction

Therefore the strategic metric is not simply:

100h → 70h

but:

> Human Dependency ↓ while Capability Coverage and Quality remain >= baseline.

## 10. Shared Capability advantage

Conventional small/mid-size internal IT may require a limited number of generalists to cover many domains.

Candidate atLIB model:
- Service Desk / Operator
- Infrastructure
- Microsoft 365 / Identity
- Security
- Network
- Service Management
- IT Consulting / Management

These capabilities are pooled and invoked according to Work rather than sold as dedicated person-months.

Customer-facing proposition should NOT be:

> We provide X people / Y hours.

Candidate proposition:

> We provide the IT capabilities the company needs, while continuously reducing unnecessary Human Work through FACTACT and KAIZEN.

## 11. Initial ICP hypothesis

Strong candidate characteristics, regardless of exact employee count:

- IT environment has reached a complexity that exceeds one generalist's sustainable coverage
- recurring Account / Device / SaaS / License / Infrastructure operations exist
- multiple systems/tables/vendors create duplicate administration or context search
- specialist capability is needed intermittently but not enough to justify full-time specialists in every domain
- management wants IT improvement, not merely cheap helpdesk labor
- meaningful IT investment exists, making better IT decisions economically valuable
- enough work is standardizable / connectable / evidence-producing
- customer accepts service-based delivery rather than named dedicated personnel

Weak candidate characteristics:
- overwhelmingly synchronous L1 inquiry work
- high physical onsite dependence
- customer requires dedicated named staff
- extremely low IT spend/value pool
- almost no recurring structured Work
- highly bespoke environment where standardization/connectivity is minimal

## 13. Price implication

¥498k / ¥600k / ¥800k remain Price Scenarios, not employee-size plans.

A 300-person company may rationally pay more than a 50-person company even if measured atLIB Human Work is identical because:
- Work composition can require higher role capability
- responsibility/risk can be greater
- service level can be higher
- specialist pool value can be higher
- alternative TCO can be higher
- IT investment decision value can be higher

But scale alone does not justify price. The Assessment must establish the relevant FACT.
